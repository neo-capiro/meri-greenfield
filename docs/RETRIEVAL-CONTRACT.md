# Retrieval contract foundation 0.2.0

Status: additive R2 foundation, pending independent review. The [common](../packages/contracts/retrieval-0.2/common.schema.json) and [retrieval](../packages/contracts/retrieval-0.2/retrieval.schema.json) schemas establish request-context and response shapes. [validate.mjs](../packages/contracts/retrieval-0.2/validate.mjs) checks declared cross-field consistency. These checks do not authenticate, authorize, connect sources, implement retrieval or qualify a domain operation. REL-02 and REL-03 remain open.

The gateway binds Clerk identity, current authoritative user/membership and actual grants before resolving a requested subject. Model/UI requests contain requested client/entity identity, all-authorized active portfolio selection or personal scope; they cannot supply tenant/actor/grants. Ambiguous names, pronouns, denied IDs and unresolved entities require a trusted resolver and clarification rather than guessed identity. No resolver is implemented here. A server-resolved subject records canonical IDs, current tenant/actor, decision/policy revision, contributing scopes and a roster version for portfolios. A Meri portfolio is an authorized client roster; a Defense Research Portfolio or prospect is a distinct entity and does not gain new authority from its type name.

Every read, discovery/load/use, citation/read handle, replay/resume, publication and destination needs current authorization. Mailbox ownership, client walls, approved-memory lifecycle and private-user grants remain independent of tenant membership. Derived content inherits all contributing scopes; public evidence does not erase private lineage. Reusing the 0.1 Scope shape is not a new grant model. Protected resource/provider access stays disabled until source contracts and G2/G3 fixtures pass. [Source inventory](SOURCE-INVENTORY.md) and [integration boundary](INTEGRATION-BOUNDARY.md) retain the 22-domain discovery and missing-surface distinctions; availability is not assumed.

| Method | Contract meaning | Prohibited inference |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `candidate-search` | Bounded lexical/semantic/hybrid discovery results with record versions, snippets and required read handles | Top-k or an empty candidate page cannot establish total counts or absence |
| `exact` | A separately registered typed authoritative count/status/history/comparison operation | No arbitrary SQL, unrestricted tables or synthesis from candidate counts |
| `source-read` | Authorized full material source content with stable record/version/citation lineage | A snippet or opaque unread handle is not a completed decisive read |

The outer evidence envelope is closed and carries request/trace IDs, exact operation/version, server binding/subject, normalized window, validated argument digest and applied-filter digests. Those digests are audit metadata; they are not a generic filter execution language. Every source supplies authority (`authoritative`, `derived`, `approved-memory`), adapter/snapshot identity, checked/partial/unavailable/denied status, synchronization/as-of/retrieval timestamps and explicit fresh/stale/unknown state. Records carry immutable source/record versions, content hash, opaque citation ID, scopes and explicitly snippet/full-read/structured content. Citation and continuation strings are opaque identifiers, never preauthorized URLs or bearer credentials.

Outcomes are `complete`, `partial`, `empty`, `unavailable`, `denied` or `error`, with stable reason and retryability. Failure outcomes carry no result/aggregate count or negative assertion. The local validator checks outcome/reason/retry consistency, source-set accounting, impossible timestamps, record lineage and returned counts. `returnedCount` counts returned result records; a scalar aggregate is one returned result, while `data.value` and `enumerableTotal` carry its exact count. Candidate results keep `enumerableTotal:null`; unknown counts remain null. Partial output and unavailable sources are explicit, never zero by default.

Coverage includes sources requested/checked/unavailable, enumeration completeness, known enumerable total, actual returned count, truncation, history support and limitations. Continuation includes an opaque cursor, required decisive reads and remaining sources. Complete enumeration cannot coexist with truncation, a next cursor, unavailable/partial sources, remaining sources or bounded/unsupported history. A complete response additionally requires fresh sources and no unresolved decisive reads. Empty is a result shape, not automatically a claim of exhaustive absence.

An explicit absence assertion is allowed only for an empty typed exact count/history result with a known zero enumerable total, complete relevant enumeration, all requested authoritative sources checked and fresh, no truncation/cursor/remaining sources or decisive reads, and an exact relevant-source set. Exact results require authoritative sources; complete history must bind its enumerable total to all returned history records. The gateway must verify those declarations against the source receipts/query/paging protocol; JSON declarations cannot prove real coverage. Tenant denial never becomes a factual negative. Source outages, stale data, capped searches and unsupported history require qualified partial/unavailable language. Cursor/read handles must bind actor, current revision, scopes, source snapshot, normalized query/window and continuation, and be reauthorized at use; no cursor implementation is provided.

Window requests distinguish calendar week/month/year, fixed relative duration, explicit interval, fiscal year and unbounded history. Bounded normalized results use UTC inclusive start/exclusive end plus the requested timezone. Calendar weeks explicitly select Monday/Sunday policy; the checker verifies local midnight and a seven-local-day span, including DST. Month/year boundaries and increasing bounds are checked. Relative `days` means exact 24-hour durations ending at the explicit anchor, distinct from a calendar week. Fiscal selection preserves calendar ID, year, funding stage and revision. Comparisons require an explicitly compatible revision/stage/unit basis; broader cross-version/domain semantics need their own typed operation.

`assertWindowResolution` rejects basis/timezone/explicit-bound/duration/week-policy/FY-stage-revision substitution. It does not select the correct `this`/`last` interval from a trusted current clock, implement fiscal calendars, resolve ambiguous text or qualify a source's revision semantics. Those deterministic resolver/domain fixtures remain REL-03 work. No silent 30-day default exists in this namespace. A previously supported 0.1 request/result is not silently widened or relabeled 0.2; adapters and in-flight compatibility require separate owner review.

The current fixtures establish closed structure and consistency only. Genuine source truth, policy walls/revocation, pagination/exact aggregates, decisive passage reads, full history, SDK/proxy discovery, traces and the real 75/200-case benchmark remain open. No real request is fabricated to fill that benchmark; optional Decisions/JEV remain disabled.
