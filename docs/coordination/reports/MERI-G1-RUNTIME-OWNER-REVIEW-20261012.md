## Discovery

The accepted standalone `FixtureRegistry.revokeRun` has no non-test caller. `createFixtureControl` is the existing trusted owner composition: it instantiates the real registry, checkpoint store, handlers and ingress, exposes the registry, and owns start/close. Its close operation destroys sockets, waits for tracked promises, then releases owned object resources. It does not invoke grant revocation or provide a run-bound stop operation. The tracked handler promises disappear in `finally`; `Promise.allSettled` in cleanup does not retain a `CommitUncertain` marker for the owner after the ingress correctly reduces worker-facing failures to a bodyless refusal. A cancellation of transport is therefore not durable revocation, and completed cleanup alone cannot establish an unknown transaction outcome.

`scripts/qualification/sdk-native-evidence-owner.mjs` composes a one-shot native observer and evidence publisher. It is not an admitted-run lifecycle owner and does not invoke registry revocation or the control composition. Its existing cancellation tests inject authorization decisions and do not demonstrate durable grant invalidation. `qualification-temporal/workflow.ts` and `activities.ts` are explicitly inert fixtures with no registry/control wiring; their echoed activity outcomes are not runtime cancellation or durability evidence. Production-source search found no caller of the evidence owner or control constructors either, so this work can qualify the existing control entry point's behavior without claiming a launched/native product composition.

The author proposed an owner-only `stopRun` integrated into the existing control, with a cohesive per-binding lifecycle module if allocated by the parent. This is preferable to another unconsumed helper. Exact API and paths await parent approval; the following behavior requirements are frozen before author code.
